Geometry — Finding the area of a triangle

Area measures how much flat space a shape covers. For a triangle, choose any
side as the base. The height is the perpendicular distance from that base
line to the opposite vertex. In some obtuse triangles, the height meets an
extension of the base line outside the triangle.

Why the formula works
Two identical triangles can be arranged to form a parallelogram with the
same base and height. The parallelogram has an area of base × height, so
each triangle has an area of (base × height) ÷ 2.

Worked example
A triangle has a 12-centimeter base and a perpendicular height of 7
centimeters. Multiply 12 by 7 to get 84, then divide by 2. The area is 42
square centimeters. A sloping side may have a different length, so look
for the right angle that identifies the height. Convert both lengths to
the same unit before multiplying.

Practice question
A triangular garden covers 30 square meters and has a base of 10 meters.
How tall is the triangle? Double the area to get 60 square meters, then
divide by the base. The perpendicular height is 6 meters.

  

  

    
Earth science — Condensation and the water cycle

Water moves among the oceans, land, and atmosphere. During evaporation,
water molecules leave a liquid surface and enter the air as water vapor.
Sunlight supplies energy for much of the evaporation from lakes and oceans.
Condensation happens when water vapor changes back into liquid droplets.

How cooling produces droplets
At lower temperatures, air needs less water vapor to become saturated.
The dew point is the temperature at which air with a given amount of water
vapor becomes saturated as it cools. With further cooling, some vapor can
condense. In the atmosphere, droplets often form on tiny particles such as
sea salt or dust. Fog consists of droplets suspended near the ground.

Everyday example
A cold glass cools the air touching its outer surface. If that air reaches
its dew point, water vapor from the surrounding air can form droplets on
the glass. The droplets came from the air outside the glass.

Practice question
Why might a cold glass collect more water on a humid day than on a dry
day? Humid air contains more water vapor at the same temperature, so it
reaches saturation with less cooling.

  

  

    
Grammar — Identifying subjects and predicates

The subject of a clause tells who or what the clause is about. The predicate
contains the verb and says what the subject does, experiences, or is. In
the sentence “The students in the library have been reading quietly,” the
complete subject is “The students in the library.” The simple subject is
“students.” The complete predicate is “have been reading quietly,” and the
verb phrase is “have been reading.”

A method for finding the parts
First find the verb or verb phrase. Then ask who or what performs the action
or is being described. Include words that describe the subject when you name
the complete subject. In the example, “library” appears near the verb and
belongs to a phrase that tells which students the sentence means.

Word order can vary
In the question “Are the students ready?” the helping or linking verb can
come before the subject. Here, “the students” is the subject and “are” links
it to “ready.” In a command such as “Open your notebook,” the subject “you”
is understood.

Practice question
Find the complete subject and predicate in “Maya and Eli mapped the trail
before lunch.” The complete subject is “Maya and Eli.” The complete predicate
is “mapped the trail before lunch.”
